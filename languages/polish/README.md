# polish

Polish inflection for **any** Polish word, attested or not: noun declension,
adjectives, and verbs (non-past, past and conditional by person and gender,
imperative, participles), with a confidence for every answer.

```rust
use polish::*;

assert_eq!(noun("kot", Case::Genitive, Number::Singular), "kota");
assert_eq!(noun("ręka", Case::Locative, Number::Singular), "ręce");
assert_eq!(adjective("dobry", AdjectiveCase::Nominative, Agreement::Virile, Animacy::Animate), "dobrzy");
assert_eq!(non_past("czytać", Person::First, Number::Singular), "czytam");
assert_eq!(past("czytać", Person::First, Agreement::Feminine), "czytałam");
```

A citation gives principal parts the way a dictionary prints them: nouns
nominative and genitive singular (`dom, domu`: whether a masculine noun takes
`-a` or `-u` can't be predicted), verbs infinitive and first and second person
singular; markers name gender and aspect (`kot, kota manim`, `czytać impf`).

## How it works

This crate was generated by [flexion](https://github.com/gold-silver-copper/flexion) from a
description of Polish grammar and the Kaikki dumps of the English Wiktionary's
Polish entries.
For every part of speech, each attested word is reduced to its **inflection class**: the edits
(strip a suffix, add a suffix, maybe add a prefix, place the stress) that turn its lemma into
each of its forms. A suffix tree over lemmas, read from the last letter backwards, stores at
each node how likely each class is for words ending that way. Nodes that add nothing over
their parent are pruned; whole words survive only when they are real exceptions. To inflect a
word, the runtime walks it as deep as the tree goes and applies the most likely class that
fits. Words nobody wrote down get the patterns of the words that end like them; attested words
get their own forms.

Confidences are the tree's smoothed class probabilities. Where Wiktionary lists several
forms for one cell, each later-listed form gets half the weight of the one before, so
confidences between attested variants reflect Wiktionary's ordering, not measured usage.

## Accuracy

Measured by `cargo xtask build polish` on the Kaikki dump `Polish.jsonl` of 2026-10-03. Every number below is
computed through the same runtime this crate ships. A cell counts as right when the top
answer is the **primary** attested form: the first plain form Wiktionary lists for the
first entry with that word. Nonstandard, dialectal, dated and similar forms are never
trained on and never counted.

### Words the model never saw

A tenth of all lemmas (chosen by a hash of the lemma, so every entry of a word goes the
same way) is held out: the model is trained without them, then asked for every form they
attest. Another tenth chose the model's hyperparameters, so the held-out tenth was never
used for any decision.

the word the way a dictionary prints them (noun `mpers`/`manim`/`minan`/`f`/`n`; verb `impf`/`pf`).

### Sound laws

Learned from the training words: where edits differ only by a regular change of the stem's edge, they are one edit and a rule, so an unseen word gets the change too.

- **noun**:
  - the stem's last segment before the ending: c→ć, g→dz, k→c, n→ń, s→ś, st→ści, sł→śl, t→ci, x→ks, z→ź, zd→źdz, ć→ci, ł→l, ń→ni, ś→si, ź→zi (23669 training pairs)
  - the stem's last segment before the ending: c→ćc, ch→s, n→ńc, s→śc, st→śc, t→c, z→źc, ć→c, ł→l, ń→n, ś→s, ź→z (10731 training pairs)
  - the stem's last vowel, across its final consonants: ó→o, ą→ę (30 training pairs)
  - the stem's last segment before the ending: s→śn, z→n (29 training pairs)
- **verb**:
  - the stem's last segment before the ending: c→kl, ch→dzi, g→ż, k→cz, n→ły, sn→śli, sp→śpi, sł→śl, t→cz, w→j, z→ż, zd→żdż, zn→źli, ć→l, ść→dl (1776 training pairs)
